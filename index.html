Going, Going, Gone!

Aim of the game

Buy and sell paintings at auctions, art fairs and private sales. There are 9 collecting groups with 4 artworks each, 36 in all.

Own 3 works from one group and their value doubles. Own all 4 and it triples. The game ends when no artworks are left to buy or everyone is out of money.

Winner: highest total of artwork value + cash.

Setup

One player is the Banker and gives everyone $1.5 billion:

2 × $500M
3 × $100M
2 × $50M
3 × $20M
4 × $10M

Deal 3 artworks to each player. Everyone starts on the Venice Biennale. The Banker goes first, then play moves left, moving clockwise by the dice.

Your turn

  1. Collect $50M from the Bank — every turn after the first round.
  2. Museum business (optional): lend an artwork for cash, repay a loan, or take over an overdue one. Always before you roll.
  3. Roll the dice and move clockwise.
  4. Do what your space says — see Cards & spaces.
  5. Pass the dice to the player on your left.

Set math: why the 3rd card matters

Suppose every card in a group has the same Estimate, E. Watch the whole group’s value as you collect more.

Questions to discuss

  • Write V(n) for n = 0…4 in terms of E. Linear? Quadratic? Neither?
  • Why can it be smart to pay far more than the Estimate for one card?
  • You own 2 Pop works; a rival owns the other 2. Who wants the next one more?
  • Museum: cash now, same amount back later. A 0% loan? What is the real risk?
  • Lawyer: miss a turn, pay $200M, or destroy a $90M card you don’t need. Which is cheapest?
